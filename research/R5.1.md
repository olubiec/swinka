## Findings

Short version: the sources I could open give principles, not time-to-afford or daily-cap numbers. I found no source with "cheap = X days, big = Y days". Anything like that below is marked UNVERIFIED.

**Faucets / sinks / inflation (principles, no numbers)**
- Faucets create currency (play rewards, logins), sinks remove it (fees, consumables, repairs). Crafting-type "transformations" act as both. Inflation happens when faucet inflow exceeds sink outflow; the target is roughly net-zero flow. Deflation encourages hoarding. When money supply is too high, add attractive sinks rather than cutting player income; the most effective sinks are often the least accepted, so disguise them as gameplay. Source: reading summary of Castronova's *Virtual Economies* (secondary source, personal notes): https://www.antoinebuteau.com/virtual-economies-book-summary/
- Sources and sinks should match over time; excess sources -> inflation, excess sinks -> players starve and leave. Plan sinks to absorb shocks (events, sales, bugs). Recommends drawing the flow and using Machinations. Source: blog post (secondary): https://eyaldror.com/blog/game-economics
- "Anchor to time, not coin counts": express costs as sessions of play; design sinks first, then size sources; front-load generosity then taper; check inflow vs outflow per player segment weekly. No session targets given. Source: industry blog (secondary, opinion): https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-07-14-game-economy-design-virtual-currency-balancing/
- GDC talk "Building Sustainable Game Economies: The Three Design Pillars" (Edward Castronova, GDC 2023). The vault page describes topics (inflation, currencies, pricing, live ops) but gives no figures. Source: GDC Vault page (overview only; I could not see the talk): https://gdcvault.com/play/1028982/Building-Sustainable-Game-Economies-The

**Idle-game cost curves (the only hard numbers found)**
- Game Developer, "The Math of Idle Games, Part I": cost of the next unit grows by a factor of 1.07 per owned unit in the AdVenture Capitalist lemonade-stand example (base cost about 4). Early on, income covers the next unit every second; later the wait becomes "prohibitively long". No target wait times given. Source: Game Developer article: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-i
- Game Developer, "Balancing tips: how we managed math on Idle Idol": upgrade costs grew about 1.1x per level while rewards grew slower; cost overtook reward around level 35 in their chart. Source: Game Developer postmortem: https://gamedeveloper.com/design/balancing-tips-how-we-managed-math-on-idle-idol

**Neko Atsume (closest cozy reference)**
- 1 gold fish = 50 silver fish by one fan analysis (250 silver = 5 gold); on the wiki, 500 silver buy 10 gold in the base game (same ratio), 300 in Neko Atsume+. Source: https://medium.com/@juliaychong/data-atsume-bce69e8cbafa (fan analysis, the author's own inference) and https://nekoatsume.fandom.com/wiki/Fish (fan wiki).
- Uncollected gifts are capped at 100; oldest are auto-accepted beyond that (a soft "cap" on stockpiled income). Source: https://nekoatsume.fandom.com/wiki/Fish
- The same sources give no earn-per-day or goodie price list, so time-to-afford for Neko Atsume is not available from what I opened.
- Pou and Tamagotchi-likes: my searches returned no usable analysis of their economies. Not found.

## Answer for Cuy
No source gives reliable "days to afford" numbers for pet games, so derive them in your own 30/60-day simulation (F3-02/F7-02) and treat them as design choices.
Principles that are sourced: balance faucets and sinks to roughly net-zero, add sinks rather than cutting income, state costs as sessions/days of play, be generous early and taper.
For cost curves, exponential growth of 1.07-1.1x per level is the documented idle-game range (UNVERIFIED as a fit for a pet game).
Cap stockpiled/offline income (Neko Atsume caps uncollected gifts at 100).
