Question: Guinea pig life stages with ages (baby, juvenile, adult, senior), typical lifespan, and the list of foods that are
toxic or unsafe (onion, chocolate, others) with reasons. Veterinary sources only.

## Findings

Lifespan:
- RSPCA NSW: typically about 5-7 years (https://www.rspcansw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Guinea-Pigs.pdf).
- PetMD (not a veterinary-only source, flagged): hairless about 7-8 years, long-haired about 4 years, record 14 years (https://www.petmd.com/exotic/how-long-do-guinea-pigs-live-average-lifespan). The hairless/long-haired split looks odd; treat as UNVERIFIED.

Life stages (only source found, PetMD, flagged as not veterinary-only): newborn birth to 1 month; adolescent 1 to 6 months; adult 6 months to 4 years (full size around 6 months); senior 4 years and over. The RSPCA NSW leaflet gives no stage ages. PDSA says to avoid alfalfa hay after about six months, consistent with the adult threshold.

Unsafe foods (reasons are rarely given):
- RSPCA NSW "never feed" list, no reasons given: beans (including raw), beetroot, biscuits, bread, breakfast cereals and grains, buttercups, lily of the valley, chocolate, sweets and sugar, corn, peas, nuts, garden shrubs, onions and onion grass, pickled foods, potato tops, rhubarb leaves, seeds, spinach.
- Wood Green (https://woodgreen.org.uk/pet-advice/guinea-pig/poisonous-food-and-plants-for-guinea-pigs/): unsafe: onions, garlic, chives, leeks (no reason), avocado, potatoes and rhubarb (any part), tomato leaves and stalks, hot peppers, lemons/limes/grapefruit (too acidic), sugary/yoghurt/milk treats (cannot digest easily), dried fruit (very high in sugar), nuts and seeds (high fat, obesity, hard to digest), bulb plants such as crocus, tulip, daffodil (all parts toxic). Chocolate is not mentioned on the Wood Green page.
- Merck pet-owner nutrition page lists no foods to avoid; it only advises against multivitamins.
- Conflict: RSPCA NSW says never spinach, PDSA says calcium-rich spinach is fine in small amounts mixed with other foods.
- Onion and chocolate are named by RSPCA NSW (and onion by Wood Green) but neither gives a reason; no veterinary source with a reason (for example toxic compounds) was found.

## Answer for Cuy
Lifespan about 5-7 years (RSPCA NSW); stages from PetMD only: newborn 0-1 month, adolescent 1-6 months, adult 6 months-4 years, senior 4+ years (UNVERIFIED as a vet source).
Onion, garlic, chives, leeks, chocolate (RSPCA NSW), avocado, potato and rhubarb (Wood Green), and sugary or fatty treats are listed as unsafe; reasons are given only for acidity, sugar and fat.
Spinach is disputed between sources, so keep it out of the "toxic" list.
