Question: Guinea pig vitamin C: daily requirement in mg/kg for adults, and increased needs (pregnant, sick, young). Which common vegetables are high and low in vitamin C (mg per 100 g) — bell pepper, parsley, kale, cucumber, carrot, dandelion, strawberry, apple. Veterinary sources only.

## Findings

- Daily requirement (veterinary sources):
  - Merck Veterinary Manual, nutrition page (msdvetmanual professional version, https://www.msdvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/guinea-pigs/housing-and-nutrition-of-guinea-pigs): adults about 10–25 mg/kg/day; growing, pregnant, lactating or ill animals 30 mg/kg/day or more.
  - Merck Veterinary Manual, pet owner page (https://www.merckvetmanual.com/all-other-pets/guinea-pigs/nutritional-problems-of-guinea-pigs): minimum 10 mg/kg/day; pregnant females 30 mg/kg/day.
  - The two pages give slightly different ranges (adult 10–25 vs minimum 10). Both are Merck; use the professional figure with care.
- Vegetable vitamin C (mg/100 g): the Merck page names bell pepper, parsley and kale as high in vitamin C but gives no numbers. Numbers below come from the Norwegian food table, NOT a veterinary source (https://matvaretabellen.no/en/vitamin-c-askorbic-acid):
  - Bell pepper: 163 (yellow/orange raw), 126 (red raw), 120 (green raw).
  - Parsley: 108 (herb, raw). Note: 125 is dried parsley, not fresh.
  - Kale: 120 (raw).
  - Strawberry: 69 (raw).
  - Cucumber, dandelion, apple: NOT FOUND in that table (apple appears only in mixed products).
  - Carrot: no plain carrot entry in that table (only mixed frozen products, 15–55 mg).
- Merck pet-owner page on carrot: described as "should not be fed" (taken from the page summary; not checked line by line).
- Veterinary sources for numeric vegetable values: NOT FOUND.

## Answer for Cuy
Adults need about 10 mg/kg/day of vitamin C, and pregnant, growing or sick guinea pigs about 30 mg/kg/day (Merck Veterinary Manual).
High-vitamin-C vegetables include bell pepper (120–163 mg/100 g) and kale (120 mg/100 g); parsley is about 108 mg/100 g (fresh herb).
These numbers are from a Norwegian food table, not a vet source, so flag them as not veterinary. Cucumber, carrot, dandelion and apple lack reliable figures.
