Question: Guinea pig vitamin C: daily requirement in mg/kg for adults, and increased needs (pregnant, sick, young).
Which common vegetables are high and low in vitamin C (mg per 100 g) — bell pepper, parsley, kale, cucumber, carrot,
dandelion, strawberry, apple. Veterinary sources only.

## Findings

Requirement (veterinary):
- Merck Veterinary Manual, professional version (https://www.msdvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/guinea-pigs/housing-and-nutrition-of-guinea-pigs): adults about 10-25 mg/kg/day; growing, pregnant, lactating or ill animals 30 mg/kg/day or more.
- Merck Veterinary Manual, pet-owner version (https://www.merckvetmanual.com/all-other-pets/guinea-pigs/nutritional-problems-of-guinea-pigs): at least 10 mg/kg/day; pregnant females 30 mg/kg/day; practical figures about 10 mg per animal per day and 30 mg for a pregnant female.
- The two Merck pages give different adult ranges (10-25 vs minimum 10); the wider range is from the professional page.
- PDSA names red bell peppers as a regular vitamin C source but gives no mg/kg numbers (https://www.pdsa.org.uk/what-we-do/blog/five-common-health-problems-in-guinea-pigs-and-how-to-prevent-them).
- RSPCA NSW (https://www.rspcansw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Guinea-Pigs.pdf): guinea pigs cannot make vitamin C; suggests small amounts of citrus and kiwi, plus fresh leafy greens and herbs; gives no quantities.

Vegetables (no veterinary source gives mg per 100 g; Merck only calls bell pepper, parsley and kale high in vitamin C and names no values):
- Non-veterinary food tables, flagged as such:
  - Norwegian food table (https://matvaretabellen.no/en/vitamin-c-askorbic-acid), mg/100 g raw: bell pepper 126 (red), 120 (green), 163 (yellow/orange); parsley herb 108; kale 120; strawberry 69. No plain cucumber, dandelion, apple or carrot entry.
  - USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy via a mirror (https://getfoodfacts.com/food/cucumber-with-peel-raw-168409): cucumber with peel, raw 2.80 mg/100 g (FDC 168409).
  - USDA NAL table (https://nal.usda.gov/sites/default/files/page-files/Vitamin%20C.pdf), household measures only: kale raw 19.6 mg per cup; carrots raw 7.6 mg per cup chopped; apples raw without skin 4.4 mg per cup sliced. Cannot be converted to per 100 g without cup weights.
- Dandelion: no vitamin C value found.
- Merck professional page also says carrots and berries "should not be fed" (from my reading of the page summary; check the exact wording before using it for carrot or strawberry).

## Answer for Cuy
Adults about 10-25 mg/kg/day; growing, pregnant, lactating or ill guinea pigs 30 mg/kg/day or more (Merck Veterinary Manual).
High: bell pepper (about 120-163 mg/100 g), parsley (about 108), kale (about 120), strawberry (about 69); low: cucumber (2.8), apple and carrot (a few mg).
These per-100 g values are from food tables, not vet sources; dandelion has no value; Merck wording on carrot and berries needs checking.
