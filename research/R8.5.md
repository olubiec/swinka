Question: Guinea pig daily activity: time spent resting/sleeping (nap length), eating, exploring, grooming, playing;
crepuscular activity; how often adults "popcorn"; meaning of wheeking, purring (content vs annoyed), rumbling, chutting,
teeth chattering. Compare with this budget and say which numbers are supported: rest 50 %, eating/drinking 28 %,
sniffing 8 %, walking 8 %, grooming 3 %, play 3 %; sleep 4-6 h/day in short naps; popcorn 1-3/day for adults.

## Findings

- Sleep: PetsRadar, "Do guinea pigs sleep?" with vet tips (https://www.petsradar.com/advice/do-guinea-pigs-sleep-five-tips-from-a-vet-for-keeping-your-guinea-pig-healthy): about 4-6 hours a day, quoted from owner reports. The article calls them diurnal, sleeping mostly at night but not all night. Nap length is not given. Source type: media article, not a veterinary study.
- Crepuscular: no veterinary or welfare-organisation source found; the PetsRadar article says diurnal, so the question's premise is unconfirmed.
- Time budget (eating, exploring, grooming, play in % of the day): not found in any source. The only quantitative behaviour study found, Elsbacher et al., Animals 2025, 15(8):1157 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12024334/), is a survey of 1,181 German-speaking caretakers (June-September 2020) rating how often behaviours occur, not time spent.
- Popcorning: the same study ("locomotor play") was reported several times per day for 19.9% of the focus animals and at least daily for 26.8% (owner recall, ages not separated). PetsRadar (article by Dr Joanna Woodnutt MRCVS, https://www.petsradar.com/advice/guinea-pig-noises-and-body-language-explained): jumping and running as a sign of excitement and happiness, more common in young pigs.
- Resting (same study): resting with companions several times per day for 48.1%, resting alone for 53.0%.
- Sounds (PetsRadar, vet-written): wheeking = a demanding squeak, usually wanting food or attention; purring = usually contentment, but short chattier purrs with a tense pig suggest annoyance; rumbling = low-pitched purr, dominance display (swaying, strutting), also male courtship, or dislike of a touched spot; teeth chattering = anger or annoyance, may also signal dental pain; chirping = rare, bird-like, thought to signal stress, more common in young pigs. Chutting: not mentioned in this article or elsewhere in the sources read.

## Answer for Cuy
Only "sleep 4-6 h/day" has a source (owner reports quoted by PetsRadar); none of the percentages and not "popcorn 1-3/day for adults" is supported.
Sounds: wheek = wants something, purr = content (annoyed if short and tense), rumble = dominance or courtship, teeth chatter = annoyance or pain; chutting was not found.
Keep the budget as a game design choice, not as veterinary fact.
