Question: Guinea pig daily activity: time spent resting/sleeping (nap length), eating, exploring, grooming, playing; crepuscular activity; how often adults "popcorn"; meaning of wheeking, purring (content vs annoyed), rumbling, chutting, teeth chattering. Compare with this budget and say which numbers are supported: rest 50 %, eating/drinking 28 %, sniffing 8 %, walking 8 %, grooming 3 %, play 3 %; sleep 4-6 h/day in short naps; popcorn 1-3/day for adults.

## Findings

- Sleep: PetsRadar vet-based article (https://www.petsradar.com/advice/do-guinea-pigs-sleep-five-tips-from-a-vet-for-keeping-your-guinea-pig-healthy): about 4–6 hours a day, per owner reports cited in the article. Says they are diurnal and sleep mostly at night. Nap length: NOT FOUND.
- Time budget (rest 50 %, eating 28 %, sniffing 8 %, walking 8 %, grooming 3 %, play 3 %): NOT SUPPORTED. The sleep article gives no time percentages; it gives food proportions only (about 75% hay, 10–15% fresh vegetables, about 10% pellets), which are diet, not time.
- Crepuscular: the sleep article calls guinea pigs diurnal, which conflicts with the crepuscular claim in the question. Not resolved.
- Popcorning (PetsRadar, https://www.petsradar.com/advice/guinea-pig-noises-and-body-language-explained): jumping and running, a sign of excitement and happiness; more common in young pigs. Frequency per day for adults (1–3): NOT SUPPORTED.
- Wheeking: a demanding squeak aimed at people, usually meaning the pig wants something (often food).
- Purring: usually contentment; short, chattier purrs with a tense body suggest annoyance.
- Rumbling: low-pitched, a dominance display (swaying, strutting); males also rumble to court females; during petting it can mean the spot is disliked.
- Chutting: NOT in the PetsRadar article (the article mentions only "chattering").
- Teeth chattering: clicking or gnashing signals anger or annoyance; can also point to dental pain.

## Answer for Cuy
Sleep of about 4–6 hours a day in short naps is supported (PetsRadar, citing owner reports); popcorning is excitement, not a fixed daily count.
The time-budget percentages (rest 50 %, eating 28 %, etc.) and "popcorn 1–3 per day" are NOT supported by any source read and should not go into the game.
Sounds: wheek = wants something, purr = content (or annoyed if tense), rumble = dominance, teeth chatter = annoyance or pain; "chutting" is not verified.
