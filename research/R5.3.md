Question: Achievement design for small casual/cozy games: how many achievements, ratio of easy/medium/hard, typical reward size relative to daily income, examples of achievement lists from cozy pet games. Cite sources.

## Findings

**How many, and the easy/medium/hard mix**
- RetroAchievements developer docs give a suggested (not mandatory) split for an "average" set of 50: 4 freebie, 6 easy, 10 medium, 10 medium-hard, 14 hard, 6 very hard, 0 super-hard. The page says set size should not have hard rules. Source: https://docs.retroachievements.org/developer-docs/difficulty-scale-and-balance.html (community docs for retro games, so it is a loose fit for a cozy game).
- Game Developer, "Achievement Design 101" (Greg McClanahan, 2009, about roughly 800 achievements on Kongregate): criticises sets where about 90% need serious dedication; prefers many achievements most players can earn, with very few trivial ones (trivial ones devalue the rest); avoid grind ("1000 kills") and luck-based difficulty. Source: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/achievement-design-101
- GDC Europe 2010, "Achievement Design: Lessons From an Xbox Live Community Study" (Mikael Jakobsson): the vault page is only an overview and gives no numbers. Source: https://gdcvault.com/play/1013706/Achievement-Design-Lessons-From-an

**Reward size**
- RetroAchievements point tiers: 0-5 easy, 10 medium, 25 hard, 50 very hard, 100 bonus-only. Scoring depends on difficulty, how many similar achievements exist (fewer -> higher), and game length. Source: https://docs.retroachievements.org/developer-docs/achievement-scoring.html
- These are score points, not currency. I found no source that relates an achievement's reward to a game's daily income. Whatever ratio you pick is UNVERIFIED.

**Examples from cozy pet games**
- I did not find an opened, citable list of achievements from Neko Atsume, Pou or similar. Search results pointed at Neko Atsume "mementos" guides and a Steam achievements page for another title, none of which I opened. Not found.

## Answer for Cuy
Design for a short list (a few dozen at most is fine; no source fixes a number) with a mix weighted to easy/medium rather than the RetroAchievements 50-set, which is skewed hard. Reference split to adapt: roughly 20% freebie/easy, 20% medium, the rest harder, with only a handful very hard (UNVERIFIED for cozy games).
Avoid trivial and grind achievements; reward care and discovery. Make rewards small multiples of one day's income; no source gives the ratio, so test it in the 30/60-day simulation.
