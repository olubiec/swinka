Question: Advice on writing procedural text so short daily entries do not feel repetitive (Kate Compton "So you want to build a generator", Emily Short's blog on procedural text, other well-known sources): summarise the 8 most practical tips with links.

## Findings

Kate Compton's original Tumblr essay "So you want to build a generator" could not be opened (robots restrictions). Its summary below comes from a Game Developer page on her GDC talk "Practical Procedural Generation for Everyone" and from FlowingData's pointer to the essay (link: http://galaxykate0.tumblr.com/post/139774965871/so-you-want-to-build-a-generator). Everything is paraphrased.

Eight practical tips:
1. **Write moderate-sized slots.** Slots with very few options feel samey; huge random lists feel meaningless. Aim for medium variety per slot. Emily Short, Visualizing Procgen Text Part Two: https://emshort.blog/2016/09/12/visualizing-procgen-text-part-two/
2. **Check effective odds, not option counts.** Adding a rare option next to a common one can make repetition worse; look at how often each option is actually picked. Same source.
3. **Balance branches.** Count outputs under each choice and weight so no branch dominates; tune so complex options do not crowd out simple ones. Same source.
4. **Vary sentence length and rhythm.** Repeated structure feels mechanical even when the words change; avoid producing the same shape twice in a row. Same source.
5. **Tie variation to state.** Random filler that ignores the world is noticed as arbitrary; link text to what actually happened (care actions, mood, stage). Emily Short, "Bowls of Oatmeal and Text Generation": https://emshort.blog/2016/09/21/bowls-of-oatmeal-and-text-generation/
6. **Beware "procedural oatmeal".** Many different-looking outputs that players do not perceive as different are not variety; fewer, more distinct variants beat many near-identical ones. Same Emily Short post and the Compton GDC summary: https://gamedeveloper.com/design/practical-procedural-generation-for-everyone-
7. **Hold random choices steady within one output and combine clauses.** Fixing a choice for the length of a text keeps it coherent; merging same-subject sentences avoids "X did A. X did B." chains. Emily Short, Procedural Text Generation in IF: https://emshort.blog/2014/11/18/procedural-text-generation-in-if/
8. **Curate the randomness and test.** Pure randomness often looks wrong; use structure, and brute-force many seeds and keep the good ones. Compton GDC summary (link in item 6). The same Emily Short IF post also notes that consistent wording can help when players scan results, so variety is not always the goal.

Also useful: limit recursive rules so expansion cannot run away (Emily Short, Visualizing Procgen Text Part Two).

## Answer for Cuy
Keep slots medium-sized, check real pick probabilities, and balance branches so common phrasings do not dominate.
Vary sentence shape and avoid repeating the same one twice in a row; hold random picks steady within one entry.
Pull in game state (needs, stage, recent actions) so entries differ for a reason, and prefer fewer distinct entries over many near-copies.
