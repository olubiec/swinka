Question: Recommended home health routine for pet guinea pigs: how often to weigh, what weight loss (in % or grams over what time)
should trigger a vet visit, nail trimming interval, how often a routine vet check, cage cleaning frequency,
fresh water and hay guidance. Veterinary sources only.

## Findings

- Merck Veterinary Manual, pet-owner pages (https://merckvetmanual.com/all-other-pets/guinea-pigs/routine-health-care-of-guinea-pigs and https://www.merckvetmanual.com/all-other-pets/guinea-pigs/routine-care-of-guinea-pigs):
  - Vet check: about once a year, vet experienced with guinea pigs; no vaccinations needed.
  - Nails: every few weeks; clip about 1/8 inch (0.3 cm) past the quick.
  - Cage: spot-clean every day, thorough clean once a week.
  - Hay: unlimited grass hay every day. Fresh leafy greens every day. Fresh water every day.
  - Brushing once a week; bathing only when dirty or matted.
  - Weighing: no guidance on the page. Loss of appetite or weight is listed as a reason to see a vet promptly.
- Wood Green (UK animal charity, https://woodgreen.org.uk/pet-advice/guinea-pig/your-guinea-pigs-health-what-to-look-out-for):
  - Full health check weekly (weight is part of it), behaviour check daily; use small kitchen scales.
  - Nails every 4-6 weeks once the animal is about one year old.
  - Typical adult weights: males 800-1200 g, females 800-1000 g.
  - Hay about 85% of the diet; avoid muesli mixes.
  - Weight loss is a warning sign for dental problems, mites and urinary problems: contact a guinea-pig-savvy vet.
- PDSA (https://www.pdsa.org.uk/what-we-do/blog/five-common-health-problems-in-guinea-pigs-and-how-to-prevent-them): trim nails regularly, check feet often, clean enclosure regularly with low-dust bedding, fresh water refreshed regularly; weight loss is a warning sign; no weighing schedule.
- RSPCA NSW (https://www.rspcansw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Guinea-Pigs.pdf): check toenail length during daily grooming; see a vet if the animal is quieter than usual, eats or drinks differently, or has changes in droppings or urine, or loses hair patches.
- Weight loss threshold in % or grams over a time: not found in any veterinary or welfare-organisation source. Owner forums (for example theguineapigforum.co.uk) discuss thresholds but are not veterinary sources and were not used.

## Answer for Cuy
Vet check about yearly, nails every few weeks (Merck) or every 4-6 weeks (Wood Green), spot-clean daily and full clean weekly, unlimited hay, fresh greens and water daily.
Weigh as part of a weekly health check (Wood Green); any weight loss is a reason to contact a vet, but no source gives a number (% or grams).
Do not hard-code a weight-loss percentage as veterinary advice.
