Question: For these guinea pig conditions give 3-5 early signs an owner can see and the urgency (emergency today / vet within
days / monitor): scurvy, dental malocclusion (overgrown teeth), bumblefoot (pododermatitis), mites/mange, respiratory infection,
gastrointestinal stasis, bloat. Veterinary sources only.

## Findings

Sources: Merck Veterinary Manual noninfectious diseases (https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/guinea-pigs/noninfectious-diseases-of-guinea-pigs), Merck pet-owner nutrition and skin pages, PDSA (https://www.pdsa.org.uk/what-we-do/blog/five-common-health-problems-in-guinea-pigs-and-how-to-prevent-them), Wood Green (https://woodgreen.org.uk/pet-advice/guinea-pig/your-guinea-pigs-health-what-to-look-out-for), RSPCA Australia (https://kb.rspca.org.au/knowledge-base/what-are-common-health-problems-in-guinea-pigs/), PetMD GI stasis article reviewed by a DVM (https://www.petmd.com/exotic/gi-stasis-in-guinea-pigs).

| Condition | Early signs | Urgency as stated by sources | Suggested bucket |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scurvy | reduced appetite or trouble picking up food, rough coat, limping/weakness, swollen joints (Merck); hair loss, loose teeth (RSPCA AU) | no timeframe given | vet within days (my mapping, UNVERIFIED) |
| Dental malocclusion | mild loss of appetite then not eating, trouble chewing, drooling, weight loss (Merck); longer or missing tooth, angled wear (Wood Green) | RSPCA AU: vet "as soon as possible"; Wood Green: contact a guinea-pig-friendly vet | vet within days, sooner if not eating |
| Bumblefoot | redness, ulcers, callus-like swellings (Merck); sore pressure areas, nails/spurs (PDSA) | vet assessment; no timeframe; untreated can reach joints and bone | vet within days (UNVERIFIED mapping) |
| Mites/mange | hair loss, intense itching, inflamed skin on thighs/shoulders/neck (Merck); red flaky skin, scabs around face and ears (Wood Green) | severe mites can cause seizures and death (Merck); vet for diagnosis | vet within days; sooner for severe itching or seizures |
| Respiratory infection | nasal discharge, laboured breathing, sneezing, coughing, wheezing, lost appetite (PDSA) | PDSA: "High" urgency, can become fatal quickly, vet as soon as signs appear | emergency / same day |
| GI stasis | eating less or not at all, fewer or no droppings, low energy, hunched posture, teeth grinding (PetMD, DVM-reviewed; the article notes AI assistance plus human review) | PetMD: medical emergency, can be fatal within 24-48 hours | emergency today |
| Bloat (gastric dilation/volvulus) | early signs often missed; later severe depression, lying down, distended abdomen, severe pain (Merck) | Merck: generally fatal, urgent treatment of shock and pain | emergency today |

- Merck does not describe mites and GI stasis on the noninfectious-diseases page; the mite data come from the Merck skin page (https://www.merckvetmanual.com/all-other-pets/guinea-pigs/skin-problems-of-guinea-pigs).
- Only respiratory infection, GI stasis and bloat have explicit urgency wording; "vet within days" for the other four is my own bucketing because sources give no timeframe.

## Answer for Cuy
Emergency today: bloat (swollen belly, lying down, pain), GI stasis (not eating, no droppings), respiratory infection (nasal discharge, laboured breathing).
Dental problems: vet as soon as possible; scurvy, bumblefoot and mites: vet, but no source gives a timeframe (treated here as within days).
Early signs per condition are in the table; the urgency buckets for scurvy, bumblefoot and mites are UNVERIFIED.
