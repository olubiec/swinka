Question: For these guinea pig conditions give 3-5 early signs an owner can see and the urgency (emergency today / vet within days / monitor): scurvy, dental malocclusion (overgrown teeth), bumblefoot (pododermatitis), mites/mange, respiratory infection, gastrointestinal stasis, bloat. Veterinary sources only.

## Findings

- Scurvy (Merck, noninfectious diseases, https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/guinea-pigs/noninfectious-diseases-of-guinea-pigs): early signs: reduced appetite or trouble picking up food, rough coat, limping/weakness, swollen joints, slow wound healing. Urgency: not stated.
- Dental malocclusion (same Merck page): early signs: mild loss of appetite progressing to not eating, trouble chewing/swallowing, drooling, weight loss. Urgency: not stated on Merck page.
- Dental (RSPCA Australia, https://kb.rspca.org.au/knowledge-base/what-are-common-health-problems-in-guinea-pigs/): strong interest in food but reluctance to eat, dropping food, drooling that wets chin/neck, weight loss. Urgency: see a vet "as soon as possible".
- Bumblefoot (Merck): redness, ulcers, callus-like swellings, infection. Urgency: vet assessment advised, no timeframe. RSPCA: redness/swelling on the sole, can progress to infection; see a vet, no timeframe.
- Mites (RSPCA): itching, scratching, fur loss, skin torn by scratching; intense scratching can cause seizures. Urgency: vet should diagnose, no timeframe stated.
- Bloat (Merck): early signs are often missed; later signs include severe depression, lying down, distended abdomen, severe pain. Called "generally fatal"; shock and pain relief are urgent. Urgency: EMERGENCY (the only condition with real urgency in these sources).
- Respiratory infection: NOT COVERED by any source opened.
- GI stasis: NOT COVERED as a named condition by any source opened.

## Answer for Cuy
Bloat is the only condition the sources call an emergency: a swollen belly, lying down and severe pain mean vet today.
Dental problems (drooling, not eating, weight loss) should see a vet as soon as possible (RSPCA); scurvy, bumblefoot and mites need a vet with no timeframe stated.
Respiratory infection and GI stasis were not covered by the sources read; do not assign urgency to them without a source.
